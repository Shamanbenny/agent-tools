name: concept-explainer
description: Explain unfamiliar or complex concepts in clear, structured language. Use when the user asks what something means, how it works, or asks for an explanation.
---

Explain the idea so the reader can build a correct mental model, not just repeat a short definition.

## Style

- Use ASD-STE100 principles for about 80% of the explanation: prefer common, precise words; use one main idea per sentence; keep sentences short; use active voice; name the subject before describing its action; and connect related ideas with clear transitions.
- Treat the 80% target as a writing guide, not a word-count test or a claim of ASD-STE100 compliance. Use the remaining room for natural phrasing, a useful analogy, or a technical term when it makes the idea clearer.
- Do not force every sentence into a rigid template. Keep correct technical terms, define them on first use, and preserve necessary nuance.
- Avoid unexplained acronyms, idioms, vague pronouns, and decorative language. Do not make the explanation childish or remove important details just to make it shorter.
- Use examples that match the reader's context. Mark where an analogy stops matching the real system.

## Explain in layers

1. Start with a direct one- or two-sentence definition.
2. Describe how it works in a few ordered steps or parts.
3. Give a concrete example, then connect the example back to the concept.
4. State the key boundary, exception, or common misconception if it affects understanding.
5. End with a short recap or a practical implication when useful.

Scale the detail to the question. For a simple question, use only the layers that help. For a difficult concept, build from familiar ideas to the precise explanation. If the topic is high-stakes or facts may have changed, verify the facts before explaining them.

ASD-STE100 is a technical-writing standard with controlled vocabulary and detailed rules. This skill borrows selected clarity principles; it does not promise full compliance with the standard.
